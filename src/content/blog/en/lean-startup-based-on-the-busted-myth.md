Ries opens the book by debunking a "mythmaking industry": success stories
mislead because they are built on selection bias and after-the-fact
rationalization. A few pages later, the key evidence that his method works is
the success story of IMVU. The introduction belongs to the very genre it
debunks.

The error repeats in a specific way: a success story cannot yield a causal
recipe without a denominator, the number of teams that followed the same method
and failed. The IMVU case offers no control comparison, no account of the same
startup without the method. It does have alternative explanations for success:
Steve Blank as investor and adviser, elite Silicon Valley mentors, and timing.
Symmetrically, Ries extrapolates from both his success at IMVU and his failure
("doomed from day one"). Both sides of the "myth versus method" argument rest
on single autobiographical cases.

What the case does establish is **possibility**: it rebuts the skeptic's "That
could never work!" IMVU shows that this way of working *can* work; it does not
show that it works *more reliably*. The claim that success can be engineered is
comparative and causal, so evidence of possibility is insufficient.

Ries himself offers several qualifications:

1. "You don't have to take my word for it" promises evidence throughout the
   book.
2. The movement's growth, in more than a hundred cities, across industries, and in the
   Pentagon, shows adoption rather than effectiveness and is itself exposed to
   survivorship bias.
3. The principles quietly redefine success. What they guarantee is learning,
   not a commercial result. The IMVU story can support that weaker standard of
   success as inquiry; see [A startup exists to learn how to build a sustainable business](/en/claims/startup-goal-is-to-learn/).

### What this undermines in [Startup success can be engineered: the right process can be learned](/en/claims/learnable-startup-process/)

It does not refute the claim itself. It undermines the ability of the evidence
presented in the introduction to bear that claim: the claim may be true, but the
introduction does not establish it. The practical consequence is to keep an
open ledger while reading the main chapters and track each kind of evidence:
experiment, single case, social proof, or analogy. The introduction offers no
experimental evidence at all. For a book that preaches experiments, that absence
is telling.
