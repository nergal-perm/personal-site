At Toyota, waste (*muda*) is defined against a known value stream:
overproduction, waiting, and defects are deviations from a stream that has
already been found and works. A startup has not found its value stream yet - it
does not know what will turn out to be valuable. When lean thinking is moved
into innovation, the category of waste must therefore be redefined: **the main
waste is work that produces no knowledge about what is valuable.** A product
that nobody wants is the limiting case of overproduction: every kind of resource
has been spent for zero value.

The consequence is that flawless execution of an unnecessary plan is also
waste, and the most insidious kind. The distinction between resource efficiency
and flow efficiency reaches its limit here: a team can be perfectly busy while
making no progress toward a value stream, because that stream has never been
found. Busyness simulates progress.

The introduction closes the moral circle. Labor is not the only thing being
wasted: "the passion, energy, and vision that people bring to these new ventures
are resources too precious to waste." What is at stake in applying lean to
innovation is human capacity, not unit cost.

How the claim developed: it began in the notes as my own inference (I02, "my
inference"). The *Origins* section grounds it directly: Ries states his mission
in exactly these terms, as the elimination of waste.

Parallels in the vault (the connections are made here):

- *Work in progress* is a structural safeguard against the same waste: work
  waiting outside the system remains an option and does not create sunk costs.
- [Market failure is instinctively treated with engineering remedies](/en/claims/engineering-fix-to-market-failure/) names the mechanism that produces this waste: engineering intensification instead of testing whether the work is needed.

### How this supports [A startup exists to learn how to build a sustainable business](/en/claims/startup-goal-is-to-learn/)

If waste is work without knowledge, the only systematic way to remove it is to
make the production of validated knowledge the goal of the activity. Redefining
waste and redefining the goal are two sides of the same move.
