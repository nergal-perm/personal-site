Ries gives three obvious answers to the question "What does a startup exist
for?": make a product, make money, or serve customers. He rejects all three in
favor of a fourth: **learn** how to build a sustainable business. This is a
teleological move. Learning is not a means or a side effect here; it is the
purpose of the organization.

That learning is validated scientifically through frequent experiments that
test each element of the vision. "Scientifically" means falsifiable claims and
objective measures of behavior, not laboratory rigor. [ET] Validated learning
is an **observable** reduction in uncertainty. Observability is what separates
it from self-persuasion - *we learned a lot* - and makes it something the team
can show as progress to the people who hold the innovator accountable. This is
the bridge to innovation accounting in principle 5.

The move is load-bearing because it has three direct consequences:

- **The unit of progress changes.** Startup progress is validated knowledge,
  not features, releases, or revenue. That leads directly to the need for a new
  accounting system: conventional reporting does not register this progress,
  while vanity metrics imitate it.
- **The status of the product is reversed.** If learning is the goal, the
  product is a vehicle for an experiment, a question put to the market rather
  than an answer. An early, bad MVP stops being an embarrassment and becomes an
  instrument of inquiry.
- **The cost of failure is recalculated.** A failed experiment produces
  knowledge and is therefore not a loss. The real loss is work that produces no
  knowledge: waste in the lean sense.

There is a boundary. The connection to commercial success is instrumental, not
severed: the learning is *about* a business. But success as inquiry is not the
same as company survival. A team can learn impeccably that its business is
dead. This teleology makes the strong claim that "success can be engineered"
defensible, but only by weakening success almost to a tautology. The gap between
the commercial success the book appears to promise and the research success its
method can guarantee remains part of the book's rhetoric.

### How this supports [Startup success can be engineered: the right process can be learned](/en/claims/learnable-startup-process/)

It supplies the meaning of "success" under which the engineering claim can be
defended. The process can reliably produce validated knowledge; it cannot
guarantee a commercial result. Without this redefinition, the claim is exposed
to a simple counterexample: a perfect process and a dead product.

Parallels in the vault (the connections are made here):

- *The result of a project is not a working product but a situation in which it
  is used* makes the same move at a personal scale. The result of an activity is
  redefined through a higher-order telos, and the artifact is demoted to a
  carrier of value. For Ries, the product carries an experiment; there, a
  working product carries future situations of use. The practical filters also
  match: requiring a situation of use before accepting a project plays the same
  role as a falsifying experiment before accepting a feature.
- *The Fifth Discipline*, *The five disciplines of learning organizations*, and
  *Defining a learning organization* point to Ries's startup as Senge's learning
  organization taken to its limit: learning is not one discipline among others,
  but the telos itself. My own definition exercise already contains traits of a
  Riesian startup: "people are free to experiment, take risks, and openly assess
  results"; "a jungle of ideas ... experiments first - we use the knowledge
  that is hardest to dispute."
- *Adaptation to the environment depends on objective perception* and
  *Objectivity as falsifiability* ground the word "validated": objectivity means
  being open to falsification, in Schon's sense. They also yield a less obvious
  consequence. Bad mental models survive where the price of error is too small
  to eliminate them. In a startup the cost of error is as high as it can be -
  the death of the company - but delayed, so it fails to teach. Validated
  learning installs an
  **artificial selection mechanism**: cheap, fast falsifications through
  experiments instead of one expensive, late falsification by the market.
- *Fast feedback is a universal advantage* is adjacent to the claim. The telos
  tells us *what* to maximize, validated knowledge; Build-Measure-Learn and OODA
  tell us *how*, by making fast and frequent feedback a competitive advantage.
- *Strategizing* makes the same move at the personal level: strategy is a
  continuous cycle of proposing and testing hypotheses, and a personal project
  exists in part to test a strategic hypothesis.
