Following an explicit, testable method systematically improves the chances of
success **compared with acting on a popular myth of success**: the story that
talent, hard work, and the right product will carry you to the goal on their own.

The claim has to remain comparative rather than absolute:

- The load-bearing structure is "method versus myth," not method by itself.
  - Following a method does not automatically improve the odds. Exact adherence
    to a bad method makes them worse: cargo cults and flawless execution of the
    wrong plan are still failures.
  - An explicit, testable method does provide one decisive advantage: feedback
    can be used to improve the method itself.
- The comparison class that makes the claim falsifiable is action guided by the
  field's popular narrative of success.

There is also a condition of applicability. The environment must have a
knowable structure and accessible feedback, the Kahneman-Klein condition for
valid expertise, or the method must create that feedback itself. Ries's
Build-Measure-Learn loop does the latter: it turns a chaotic startup environment
into one that can gradually be known.

Examples:

- [Startup success can be engineered: the right process can be learned](/en/claims/learnable-startup-process/) is Ries's version: the myth of the "right product" set against an engineered process.
- The Dreyfus model of skill acquisition begins with a novice following rules
  without qualification; intuition becomes legitimate only at later stages of
  mastery.
- The School of Systems Management makes the same claim in its introductory
  lesson on self-development practices: its best students trust the instructor
  and follow the form precisely; champions are made through disciplined
  adherence to form.

The warning is that this claim is vulnerable to the same selection bias used
against the success myth. There can be a *myth of method*. The defensible form
is narrower: a method can improve the probability of success and the speed of
learning, but it cannot guarantee the result.
